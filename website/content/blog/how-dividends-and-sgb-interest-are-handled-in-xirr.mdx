If you hold dividend-paying stocks or Sovereign Gold Bonds, you've probably wondered: *does my XIRR account for the dividends and interest I receive?*

It's a fair question — and the answer depends entirely on one thing: **what you do with that money after you receive it.**

## Why Dividends and SGB Interest Are Different

When a company pays a dividend or the RBI credits your SGB interest, the money lands directly in your **bank account** — not your broker account. This is important because xirrledger calculates your XIRR from your broker's fund statement, which only captures money moving in and out of your broker account.

So if dividends and SGB interest bypass your broker entirely, are they missing from your XIRR?

**Only if you don't reinvest them.**

## The Reinvestment Principle

Here's how it works in practice:

**If you reinvest the dividend/interest:**
- You transfer that money back to your broker
- It shows up as a **deposit (Bank Receipt)** in your fund statement
- xirrledger picks it up automatically — no manual entry needed
- Your XIRR correctly accounts for this additional capital ✅

**If you spend the dividend/interest or let it sit in your bank:**
- It never appears in your fund statement
- Your XIRR is calculated without it
- Your XIRR will be **slightly understated** — but that's an accurate reflection of what your broker account achieved ✅

Both outcomes are honest. The XIRR xirrledger calculates answers a specific question: *"What return did I earn on every rupee I moved into my broker account?"*

## A Concrete Example

Say you hold 100 shares of a stock that pays ₹10/share as dividend — ₹1,000 total.

**Scenario A — You reinvest:**
- ₹1,000 hits your bank → you transfer it to Zerodha → you buy more stock
- Fund statement shows a ₹1,000 deposit
- XIRR treats this as additional capital deployed on that date ✅

**Scenario B — You use it for a vacation:**
- ₹1,000 hits your bank → it's spent
- Fund statement shows nothing
- Your broker portfolio never saw that money — and your XIRR correctly reflects that ✅

## What About SGB Interest Specifically?

SGB pays ~2.5% per year as semi-annual interest directly to your bank. The SGB bond itself stays in your Zerodha holdings at its current market value.

- **Current SGB value** → already included in your portfolio value ✅
- **SGB interest received** → if reinvested, captured as a deposit ✅; if not reinvested, outside the scope of your broker XIRR ✅

The purchase outflow (when you bought the SGB via RBI/bank) is the one thing that doesn't appear in the fund statement — since it never went through your broker. That's why xirrledger lets you add SGB purchases as manual entries.

## Why We Don't Track Dividends Separately

Some calculators ask you to manually log every dividend received. We deliberately don't do this.

Here's why: if you reinvested the dividend, it's already in your fund statement as a deposit. If you log it separately *and* it shows as a deposit, you've counted that money twice — which would **understate your XIRR** (making it look like you deployed more capital than you did).

By anchoring to the fund statement, xirrledger stays clean and free of double-counting by design.

## The Bottom Line

Your XIRR from xirrledger answers: *"What return did I earn on every rupee I consciously put into my broker account?"*

Dividends and SGB interest that you reinvest become part of that answer automatically. Dividends you spend are income you enjoyed outside your portfolio — and your XIRR is none the worse for not knowing about them.

The most accurate thing you can do is **reinvest your dividends and interest**, then let the fund statement tell the full story.
